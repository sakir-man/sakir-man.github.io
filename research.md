My research program connects **Islamic astronomy, empirical observation, atmospheric conditions, and Islamic legal reasoning**. The central question is how astronomical evidence can improve the scientific reliability and legal interpretation of crescent visibility and Hijri calendar determination.

## Twilight (*syafaq*) and crescent visibility

I study the relationship between twilight sky brightness and the detectability of the young crescent Moon. The research focuses on contrast between the crescent and the sky background, atmospheric transparency, aerosols, humidity, air mass, and changing twilight conditions.

Key themes include:

- twilight sky brightness and crescent–background contrast;
- atmospheric parameters affecting visibility;
- lunar altitude, elongation, age, and illuminated fraction;
- empirical visibility thresholds in tropical environments.

## Observational astronomy and photometry

A second strand of my work develops empirical methods for crescent observation. I am particularly interested in digital imaging, aperture photometry, sky-quality measurement, and comparisons between naked-eye, optical-aided, and imaging-based observations.

This work seeks to improve the reproducibility of rukyat by connecting observational reports with measurable astronomical and atmospheric parameters.

## Hisab–rukyat and Hijri calendar systems

I examine how astronomical calculation (*hisab*) and observation (*rukyat*) can be integrated within contemporary Hijri calendar criteria. This includes the scientific evaluation of visibility criteria, uncertainty in lunar observation, regional and global calendar models, and the relationship between prediction and actual observation.

## Islamic law and astronomical evidence

My research also addresses the jurisprudential implications of scientific developments in Islamic astronomy. I study how empirical visibility data, probabilistic models, and digital observation can inform the interpretation of rukyat in Islamic law.

Topics include:

- epistemic authority of astronomical calculation and observation;
- fiqh responses to digital and imaging-assisted rukyat;
- *maqāṣid al-sharī‘ah* and calendar policy;
- the relationship between scientific evidence and Islamic legal reasoning.

## Technology and future directions

Future work explores probabilistic visibility mapping, image processing, AI-assisted crescent detection, and standardized observation networks. The aim is not to replace human judgment, but to strengthen the evidentiary basis of lunar observation and calendar decision-making.

## Research roadmap, 2025–2030

- **2025** — foundational studies and twilight (*syafaq*) data collection;
- **2026** — systematic observation, photometry, and visibility modeling;
- **2027** — testing and refinement of lunar visibility criteria;
- **2028** — integration of calendar systems, astronomical evidence, and fiqh;
- **2029** — technology development and community-based observation;
- **2030** — international collaboration and comparative visibility studies.
